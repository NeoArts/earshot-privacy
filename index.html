Earshot Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 25, 2026

Earshot lets you see what people near you are listening to, comment on their songs and chat. This policy explains what data the app uses and why.

What we collect

  • What you're playing: song title, artist and the name of the music app, read from the media player on your phone while music is playing. Earshot does not read the content of your notifications.
  • Approximate location: your position rounded to a grid of about 110 meters, so people nearby can find you. Your exact location never leaves your phone. Location is used in the background only to keep this up to date while you play music.
  • Display name that you choose.
  • Messages you send to other users and the songs you comment on.
  • Account identifier: an anonymous ID, and your Google account email if you choose to link it.
  • Blocks and reports you make.

Who can see it

  • Other Earshot users within about 1 km can see your name, the song you're playing, the music app and a rough distance (for example "< 500 m") while you're playing music.
  • Messages are visible only to the two people in the chat.
  • Reports are visible only to the Earshot team, to review abuse.

We do not sell your data or use it for advertising.

Where it's stored

Data is stored in Google Firebase (Firestore and Firebase Authentication), operated by Google LLC, and is encrypted in transit.

Your controls

  • Invisible mode (Settings) stops sharing what you play and your location immediately.
  • Block anyone so they can't message you.
  • Permissions can be revoked at any time in Android settings.
  • Delete my account (Settings) permanently deletes your profile, your chats (for both participants) and your blocked list.

Your listening status disappears automatically a few minutes after you stop playing music.

Retention

Data is kept until you delete your account. Reports may be kept up to 12 months to handle abuse.

Children

Earshot is not intended for anyone under 18.
